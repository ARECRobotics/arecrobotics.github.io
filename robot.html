02 The robot

Rules in short

Any robot that fits.
The rules are short.

One thing is fixed: your robot is driven over WiFi from a laptop or tablet. Everything else is yours to decide.

01 The one rule

If it drives, it drives over WiFi.

No Bluetooth. No radio. No cable. The AREC driver app on a laptop or tablet talks to the controller, and that is the only link allowed.

Data goes back the same way. If your robot sends battery voltage or sensor readings anywhere, it does it over the same WiFi connection.

That is the whole communication rule. Everything else about the robot is up to you: the shape, the motors, the code, the color.

How we check it

At check-in we switch the robot on and drive it. If it moves over WiFi and nothing else is paired, it passes.

02 The controller

Any board with WiFi on it.

If you can buy it and it can join a network, it counts. We do not keep a parts list, because there is no parts list.

ESP32 boards

An ESP32, ESP32-S3 or ESP32-C3 all work. WiFi is built in, there are enough pins for a motor driver, and they cost very little.

Arduino with WiFi

An Uno, Nano or Mega works if it can get on a network. A WiFi shield or module is fine.

The driver app

The official app for Windows and macOS connects to your robot over WiFi and sends the driving commands. It is the only way to drive.

Nothing else

Bluetooth and radio links are not allowed, even for data. If your robot sends something back, it goes over the same WiFi connection.

Either network

Join the venue WiFi, or let the robot make its own network and connect straight to it. Both work at every event.

Build bundle

A ready-made robot you add a controller to. Optional. Building from your own parts is just as welcome.

03 Limits

Three limits. The same for every team.

Build to the numbers, not to someone else's robot. We measure and weigh every robot before it goes on the field.

Max size
18 in on any sideIt has to fit inside an 18 × 18 × 18 inch box.
Max weight
30 lb ready to competeBattery in, everything mounted.
Max voltage
12 VNothing on the robot runs above it.
Check-in
Every robot is measured, weighed and driven before it competes.Measure yours before you travel.

If you are close to a limit, leave yourself room. The scale at an event is not kinder than the one at home.

The full rulebook comes out on December 1, alongside the game. It covers the size and weight checks, the driving rule and how matches are scored.

04 Building it

There is no right robot. There is one that fits.

This is what we would build. None of it is required, and none of it is fancy.

  • Chassis Two driven wheels and a caster is the simplest thing that works. The shape only matters if it stops you fitting the size limit.
  • Motors Any motors you can get, as long as the whole robot stays under 12 volts and 30 pounds.
  • Motor driver One that can handle the current your motors pull, and that your controller can talk to over the pins it has.
  • Controller An ESP32, or an Arduino with WiFi, running code that listens for the driver app and drives the motors.
  • Power A battery that lasts a full match, plus a charger that gets you through an event day.
  • Build bundle A ready-made robot you add a controller to. Skip the scavenging if you want; building from your own parts is just as welcome.
