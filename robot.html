The One Rule

The brain talks to your laptop or tablet over WiFi, and to nothing else.

No Bluetooth. No radio link. No serial cable for driving. The robot is driven over WiFi with the AREC driver app, and that is the whole of the communication rule. Everything else about the robot is up to you.

What Counts As A Brain

Any COTS board with WiFi on it. If it is sold to the public and it can join a network, it counts.

We do not approve a parts list, because there is no parts list. What we check at inspection is that the robot drives over WiFi and nothing else.

The ESP32 family

An ESP32, an ESP32-S3 or an ESP32-C3 will all do. WiFi is built in, there are plenty of pins for a motor driver, and they cost very little.

Arduino with WiFi

An Uno, a Nano or a Mega works as well, as long as it can reach a network. A WiFi shield or module on an Arduino is fine.

The AREC driver app

The official driver app for Windows and macOS connects to the brain over WiFi and sends the driving commands. It is the only way to drive.

No other link

Bluetooth is not allowed, and not only for driving. If the robot needs to send data back, it sends it back over the same WiFi link.

Either network

Let the robot join the venue WiFi, or let it run its own access point and connect the laptop or tablet straight to it. Both are fine, on every field we run.

Build bundle

If you would rather not start from a box of parts, the build bundle in the store is a ready made robot you can put a brain into and drive.

Size, Weight And Power

Three limits, and they are the same for everyone.

Maximum size 18 inches on a side, so a cube of 18 by 18 by 18 inches.
Maximum weight 30 pounds, measured ready to compete.
Maximum voltage Nothing on the robot runs above 12 volts.
Inspection Every robot is checked before it is allowed on the field, so it is worth measuring it yourself first.

Build to the limits, not to a reference robot. Plenty of teams have hit the weight limit and plenty have not.

The full rule set lands with the game on November 1, so you can design against it rather than guessing.

Building It

There is no right robot. There is a robot that fits the rules.

Here is what we would put together if it were our robot. None of it is required and none of it is special.

Chassis

Anything with two driven wheels and a caster will do. The shape does not matter as long as the robot fits inside 18 inches.

Motors

Any motors you can get hold of, on 12 volts or less. Keep the whole robot under 30 pounds so the motors are not carrying more than they need to.

Motor driver

One that can supply the motors you picked, and that your brain can talk to over the pins it has.

Brain

An ESP32 or an Arduino with WiFi, running the code that listens for the driver app and drives the motors.

Power

A battery that lasts a full match, and a charging setup that gets you through a whole event day.

Build bundle

The optional way to skip all of this. A ready made robot, sold through the store, that you fit a brain to. Teams who want to build from their own parts are welcome to do exactly that.
