01 The idea

The robot should decide the match, not the budget.

Every team builds its own machine, drives it the same way, and plays on the same field. You do not have to buy a kit, and there is no parts list to follow. If you can get a chassis moving, you can compete.

We keep the rules short and publish them before the season, so you can build to them instead of guessing. Size, weight, voltage and the driving rule are the only hard limits.

Everything else is yours: the chassis, the motors, the shape, the code. Some teams build from parts they already have. Others start with a build bundle. Both end up on the same field.

First robot? Start at a workshop.

We run hands-on build sessions through the season. Free for teams in the league.

02 How it works

From signup to your first match.

Four steps. We handle the field, the referees and the paperwork along the way.

01

Register

Make an account and create your team. That is the whole signup, and it costs nothing.

02

Build

Use any chassis, motors and motor driver you like. The one fixed part is the controller, and it needs WiFi.

03

Drive

Drive from a laptop or tablet with our app. Join the venue network, or let the robot make its own.

04

Compete

Sign up for the events you want and turn up. We bring the field, the referees and the game pieces.

03 What teams get

Support that comes with the season.

Workshops, mentors, the rules and help with the cost. None of it is an extra you have to ask for.

Your own design

Any chassis, any motors, any motor driver. Start from parts you already own, or buy a build bundle and add your own controller.

WiFi driving

One way to drive, for everyone. No Bluetooth, no radio, no cables trailing across the field.

Build workshops

Hands-on sessions where we help you get a robot moving. No experience needed, and free for teams in the league.

Rules you can build to

Size, weight, voltage and the driving rule, published before the season starts.

Real events

Community and official events with referees, scores and a game piece you can print at home.

Help with costs

Sponsorship covers parts, workshop space and entry fees, so the price of a robot does not decide who gets to play.
